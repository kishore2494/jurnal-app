## 1. Title and short description: change both

**Title: `Daylog: Lifelog, Mood & Habits`** (30/30)
- Alt A: `Daylog: Habit Tracker, Lifelog` (30). Keeps the exact phrase "habit tracker" but drops "mood".
- Alt B: keep `Daylog: Habit Tracker & Mood` (28). Changes nothing, but it hasn't ranked.

Why:
- The title is the heaviest field, and Play has no keyword field ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/app-store-optimization-aso-checklist-for-google-play?format=md)). In every soft search above, the leading apps have the phrase word for word in their title.
- "Lifelog" is the softest search we measured and describes v251 accurately. "Mood" keeps the soft "lifelog mood" and "photo journal mood" searches.
- The short description keeps "habit tracker". Google asks titles to be "unique… avoid common terms" ([best practices](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en)); "Daylog" is the unique part.

**Short description: `Private habit tracker, mood log, photo & voice journal and screen time. Offline.`** (80/80)
- Alt A: `Habit tracker & mood journal with photos, voice notes and screen time. Offline.` (79)
- Alt B: `Log habits, mood, photos and voice notes. See what screen time costs you.` (73). Benefit-led; use it as the challenger in an experiment.

It drops "focus timer", which we can't win (Forest owns it). It has no "free", "No Ads", "best", "#1", "new", emoji or ALL CAPS ([metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)).

## 2. Full description: 3,296 / 4,000 characters

Keyword counts: "habit tracker" ×3, "screen time" ×5, "daily log" ×3, "mood journal" ×3, "lifelog" ×2. Photos and voice notes are in paragraph 2, and screen time vs mood is in paragraph 3. The text opens by saying what the app is and who it's for, because Ask Play reads the top of the description first ([AppTweak I/O 2026](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-i-o-2026-aso-updates?format=md), [Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/google-ask-play/)).

```text
Daylog is a private lifelog for your days: a habit tracker, mood journal and daily log that also measures your screen time, then shows you how each one affects the others. It works offline, with no account.

New: photos and voice notes. Add pictures and a spoken note to any day's log or to any note. They are stored only on your phone and saved in your backup file, so one day can hold what you did, how it felt, what it looked like and what you said about it. A photo journal and a voice diary, inside your daily log.

Screen time vs mood, worked out on your phone
Daylog measures your phone use automatically and sets it against your mood, sleep and habits. The insights come from your own data and are computed on the device:
• "Heavy screen days cost you −1.2 mood"
• "Your sleep sweet spot is 7–8h: mood 7.8 vs 5.9 after short nights"
• "Walking today → mood +0.7 tomorrow" (next-day effects, with a confidence rating)
Every insight waits for enough days to be honest, and compares you only with your own averages.

Habit tracker with streaks, skip days and a strength score
A daily checklist with streaks, 90-day heatmaps and consistency stats. It is a streak tracker that forgives rest days: tap a habit twice to skip it and the streak stays alive. Weekly streaks count any 3 days. Each habit gets a strength score out of 100, a weighted trend, so one missed day can't erase months. Add a when-and-where cue ("after my morning coffee, at my desk") that shows until you tick it off, and set goals over a week, a month or a year.

Mood journal and private journal
Tap the mood meter grid to set mood and energy in one go, then pick the emotion words that fit. Write a journal entry by typing or with voice typing, add #tags, attach photos or a voice note, and search everything you have ever written.

Steps, sleep and screen time, tracked for you
Screen time is measured automatically, and with Health Connect your steps and sleep land in the same log. It also works as a quiet app usage tracker, and every automatic signal has its own on/off switch.

A one-tap 24-hour time tracker
Tap an activity when you start (work, travel, gym, scrolling) and tap the next when you switch. Your day becomes a colour-coded 24-hour timeline.

Focus timer, gym log, money tracker, calendar and notes
Pomodoro and focus timers with an ongoing notification. A workout log with per-set logging. A simple money tracker. A calendar, notes, plans and reminders. All in one app, all offline.

Look back
Year in pixels, a month-in-review deck, "on this day" entries from a week, a month or a year ago, a trophy case of awards, and share cards drawn on your phone.

Private by design
• No account, no sign-up
• No ads, no trackers, no analytics
• Your log, photos and voice notes stay on your phone
• One backup file holds everything, photos and voice notes included; export to JSON, CSV or a PDF report
• Nothing is locked. Optional tips help keep Daylog going.

Daylog keeps no cloud copy, so uninstalling erases your data. Keep your backup file somewhere safe.

Make it yours
Light, dark and black themes, a side menu with the tabs you choose, and every field, habit and section can be renamed or hidden.

Daylog: habit tracker, mood journal, daily log and screen time tracker in one private, offline lifelog.
```

Honesty notes:
- "No sync" is deliberately gone: the data-safety notes list an optional sync to the user's own Google Sheet. "Stays on your phone" and "keeps no cloud copy" are true either way.
- No AI, no "free", no competitor names, and no "subscription", because a monthly tip exists.
- Both example insights (screen days, walking) were checked: the app produces them.

## 5. Other changes worth making now

- **Reply to every review within 48 hours.** Reviews feed Play's AI "Users are saying" summary ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/play-store-ai-generated-review-summaries-3611995/)). Replying to a negative review lifts its rating by about +0.7★ (a Google figure [cited by Appbot](https://appbot.co/best-practices/how-to-reply-to-app-store-reviews/)). Effort: 10 min/day. Impact: high.
- **Change the developer name from `kishore_` to a brand, e.g. "Daylog Apps".** It's indexed but carries little ranking weight ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/app-store-optimization-aso-checklist-for-google-play?format=md)); the gain is mostly trust. It may trigger a re-review. Effort: 10 min. Impact: low–medium.
- **Make the website match the listing.** Use the same feature names in `landing.html`, because Ask Play draws answers from developer websites ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-i-o-2026-aso-updates?format=md)). Effort: 30 min. Impact: medium.
- **Run store listing experiments.** First test screenshot #1. Short descriptions can only be tested in a *localized* experiment ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12053285?hl=en)): run the recommended version against Alt B in en-US. Results will take weeks at current traffic. Effort: 1 hour. Impact: medium over time.
- **Add a custom store listing for India.** Lead with the ₹ money tracker and the photos-and-voice log. You can have up to 50 custom listings; keyword-targeted ones come later, once those terms bring visitors ([Play Console](https://play.google.com/console/about/customstorelistings), [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/aso-blog/google-i-o-2024-highlights-ai-google-play-updates)). Effort: 1–2 hours. Impact: medium.
- **Add Hindi and Tamil listings only after the app's own text is translated.** Research native keywords instead of translating, and get a native speaker to review ([AppTweak India](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/how-to-localize-your-app-in-india)). Until then, the listing must say the app is in English. Effort: medium. Impact: low–medium.
- **Use promotional content (LiveOps) if eligible.** It's limited by eligibility criteria ([Google](https://play.google.com/console/about/programs/liveopsbeta/)). If Daylog qualifies, run a 1 January "Fresh start" event. Effort: 1 hour. Impact: medium if eligible.
- **Track rankings monthly.** Check the Console's search-terms report and re-run this check, expecting day-to-day movement as Play's AI-driven results shift. Effort: 30 min/month. Impact: shows whether the title change worked.
